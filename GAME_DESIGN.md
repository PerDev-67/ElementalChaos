# Game design

## Level 1 · Hydrogen Awakening

Chapter 1 begins with a defense mission on a raised platform surrounded by three hostile portals. The commander starts just south of the core. Keep both alive for 100 seconds to unlock Hydrogen. The battle ends immediately if either runs out of health, including on the final simulation step.

| Time elapsed | Wave | Pressure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–34 seconds | First contact | Raiders with occasional skirmishers |
| 34–70 seconds | Second assault | Shorter spawn intervals and the first brutes |
| 70–100 seconds | Final surge | Faster reinforcements from the same three lanes |

Raiders and brutes head for the core and engage nearby defenders. Skirmishers pursue the commander or allies at a greater distance and attack from farther away. Shapes and colors distinguish all three. Damage flashes, strike lines, a pulse ring, and HP bars identify hits and injuries. Defeated units disappear.

The commander begins with 64 energy and regenerates 3.2 energy per second up to 100. Each enemy defeated returns 2 energy. Summoning costs 16 energy, with up to 16 allied units alive. Pulse costs 18 energy and hits enemies within 5 arena units of the commander. Squad upgrades cost 42 and 58 energy, increasing HP, damage, and attack rate. Ranks reset for each run.

| Order | Squad behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Defend | Form around the core and intercept approaching enemies |
| Follow | Stay near the commander and engage nearby enemies |
| Attack | Seek targets anywhere on the field |
| Move | Rally at the selected point and defend its immediate area |

Training uses the same controls in an arena without enemies. It introduces movement, summoning, orders, and Pulse in sequence. Entering the mission starts a fresh run. Players can skip training from its panel or begin directly from the title screen.

## Long-term design

The eventual campaign spans seven chapters and 118 sequential element unlocks. Later work can add element-specific behavior, chemistry, temperature and phase changes, larger battlefields, more mission types, and permanent progression. Level 1 uses a single balancing profile to establish the combat loop.
